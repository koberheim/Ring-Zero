# RING ZERO — External critique and improvement plan

**Date:** 2026-09-09
**Reviewer:** External critic pass, commissioned by Kevin
**Build reviewed:** `release/v1.0-candidate` @ `ebeaf72` (v1.0.0-rc2), evidence in `docs/reviews/presentation-v2/`
**Audience:** Astra, to plan from. This document deliberately does **not** edit BUILD-PLAN.md, TASKS.md or DECISIONS.md — Astra owns those. Task and decision IDs proposed here start at **T-081** and **D-129**.

---

## 1. The verdict

The game is **feature-complete and presentation-incomplete**, and those are not the same thing. Every system in the spec exists and works. What is missing is the layer that separates a working prototype from a product: **light, motion, sound, and information design.** All four are absent or vestigial, and their absence is structural, not cosmetic. You cannot polish your way out of it with better textures.

The single sentence diagnosis:

> **RING ZERO renders a correct picture of its own state, and never once performs.**

Nothing moves that isn't a simulation entity. Nothing is lit. Nothing is heard. Every screen shows you the truth and none of them make you feel it. The result reads as amateur not because the craft is bad in any one place — the ring hardware at close zoom is genuinely handsome — but because the entire transient layer of a game is missing, and players read that absence instantly even when they can't name it.

Four hard numbers that carry the whole argument:

| Measure | Count in the shipped build |
|---|---|
| `Light2D` / `CanvasModulate` / `WorldEnvironment` nodes | **0** |
| `create_tween` / `AnimationPlayer` / particle nodes in `src/presentation/` | **0** |
| Audio files in the repository | **0** (7 synthesised sine beeps + one 8-second drone) |
| Renderer | `gl_compatibility` — **glow/bloom is unavailable at all** |

A star sits at the centre of every frame in this game and it emits no light onto anything. That is the whole problem in miniature.

---

## 2. Credit where it's due

I want to be precise about what is working, because the plan below preserves it.

- **The core idea is intact and legible.** Concentric rings, clock-face bearings, radius-as-central-stat — the geometry reads. §2's thesis survives contact.
- **Close-zoom ring hardware is good.** `release-detail.png` shows truss segments, deck plating, and running lights that genuinely look like a megastructure. This is the strongest single asset in the build.
- **The engineering discipline is real.** 65 test files, deterministic simulation, polar coordinates throughout, cached fortress rendering, reproducible release probes, provenance files for fonts. The foundation is sound and the plan below builds on it rather than replacing it.
- **The information *architecture* is right even where the *design* fails.** The HUD shows the four things that matter. The build catalogue is three tabs as specified. Someone thought correctly about what to display.
- **Astra's own art gate was honest.** `docs/reviews/T-079-production-assets.md` failed the production asset gate and said so in plain language. The failure below is that RC2 shipped on top of it anyway.

## 3. Five root causes

Everything in §4 and §5 traces back to one of these. Fix these five and most of the screen-level complaints dissolve.

### RC-1 — There is no lighting model. The renderer cannot do glow.

`project.godot` sets `renderer/rendering_method="gl_compatibility"`. In Godot 4 that removes glow/bloom post-processing entirely. Every shader in the project is `render_mode unshaded`. There are zero `Light2D` nodes, zero `CanvasModulate`, zero `WorldEnvironment`.

Consequences, all visible in `release-detail.png`:

- The star does not illuminate the rings beside it. A ring at radius 1 and a ring at radius 6 receive identical light.
- Building sprites carry *baked* lighting from their AI generation, each from a different implied direction, so they never agree with each other or with the star.
- Nothing can bloom, so the star's edge is a hard cut-off and the running lights on the rings are flat dots rather than glows.
- `solar_body.gdshader` compensates with `+gold*exp(-abs(r-0.995)*170.0)*0.62` — a hand-painted hard rim — which is exactly why the star reads as a sticker pasted onto black.

This is the highest-leverage fix in the document. It is also the only one that requires a renderer change and therefore a performance re-verification.

### RC-2 — Nothing animates. The transient layer does not exist.

Grep the entire presentation layer for `create_tween`, `AnimationPlayer`, `GPUParticles2D`, `CPUParticles2D`: zero hits across all 13 files. Everything is immediate-mode `_draw()` plus shader time.

- **Ring collapse** — the most dramatic event in the game, the one §18 Q3 says must be validated before anything else gets built — is `release_view.gd:500-505`: eight identical 5-pixel diagonal line segments expanding on a perfect circle. Eight tick marks.
- **Combat hits** are a 0.2-second straight line from turret to target (`live_view.gd:981`). No muzzle flash, no impact, no death, no damage numbers, no shell casings, no tracer variation.
- **Screen shake does not exist.** `release_view.gd:122-125` computes `camera.offset = Vector2(sin(sun_phase*71), cos(sun_phase*53)) * strength` where strength maxes near 2 pixels — a smooth deterministic Lissajous wobble, not a shake. Meanwhile `application.gd:651` tells the player "Reduced motion disables camera shake." **The settings screen advertises a feature that was never implemented.**
- **No UI motion at all.** No button press, no panel transition, no number count-up, no tab slide, no hover lift.

A player's sense of production value comes mostly from transient motion. There is none here, at any layer.

### RC-3 — There is no sound design and no music.

`src/presentation/game_audio.gd` is the entire audio department. Seven sounds and one ambience, all generated at runtime as 22 050 Hz mono sine waves:

```
["ui",520],["build",180],["shot",95],["breach",48],["solar",330],["victory",440],["defeat",65]
```

- **One `shot` sound covers all five weapon types.** Flak, Mass Driver, EMP Node, Lance Emitter and Point Defense are acoustically identical.
- **One `breach` covers all destruction** — a wedge cracking and an entire ring collapsing sound the same.
- `rng.seed = 8417` is fixed, so every instance of a sound is byte-identical. No pitch or timbre variation. Guaranteed ear fatigue within ninety seconds.
- Shot audio is throttled to one per 130 ms regardless of actual fire rate, so audio desyncs from visuals the moment you have more than a few turrets.
- **There is no music.** The `victory` cue is a 1.6-second three-note sine chord. That is the payoff for a fifteen-minute run.
- There is no UI hover sound, no ring-collapse stinger, no elite-arrival cue, no boss motif, no low-core-HP warning tone, no positional audio of any kind.

For a game whose entire tension curve is "pressure rises invisibly from every bearing," having no audio channel for threat is a design failure, not just a content gap.

### RC-4 — The UI is a manually-positioned form, not a design system.

`industrial_theme.gd` is 73 lines and does far too little:

- **`CheckButton` has no stylebox and no checked/unchecked icons.** It appears only in a `font_color` loop. This is why the toggles in `release-settings.png` and `release-start.png` render as a lone grey dot floating at the right edge — Godot's default unchecked icon at the theme's scale, with no track behind it. **The player literally cannot tell what state a toggle is in.** This is the single most visible amateur tell in the build.
- **`OptionButton` has no arrow icon override**, so the dropdowns carry Godot's default chevron — the thing that makes the main menu read as an HTML form.
- **One `box()` function styles panels, buttons, sliders, popups and tabs.** A slider track, a list row and a modal panel are the same material in different colours. There is no elevation system and no hierarchy.
- **`shadow_size = 8, offset (0,5)` on everything**, including 4-pixel slider tracks and interior list rows. That is the flat "web card" look.
- **Asymmetric corner radii** (`top_left` and `bottom_right` only) are applied to every widget uniformly, so an intentional clipped-corner motif becomes noise.
- **No `Tooltip`, `ScrollBar`, `Tree`, `ItemList` or `ProgressBar` styling.** Any tooltip in this game is a default Godot grey box.

Worse, layout is **hard-coded absolute pixels at 1440p**: `application.gd` places elements at `Vector2(1500,174)`, `Vector2(240,300)`, `Vector2(144,1352)`, `Vector2(680,440)`, `Vector2(700,1070)`. `release_view.gd:135` centres the objective label with a literal `-220` fudge offset, which is why it sits visibly off-centre. This is also why an "Interface scale" dropdown had to be invented — it is a workaround for not using Godot's anchor/container system. Type sizes are magic numbers scattered across five files: 14, 24, 28, 40, 44, 48, 52, 76, 92, with no scale and no tokens. And `live_view.gd:745` draws world-space warnings with `ThemeDB.fallback_font` — **the game ships two typefaces and one of them is the engine default.**

### RC-5 — The art gate failed, and RC2 shipped on top of it.

`docs/reviews/T-079-production-assets.md` states plainly: *"The production acceptance gate does not pass."* Nine of twenty assets fail strategic-zoom identification; the elite set fails mixed-swarm separation; Relay and Repair Node fail the twelve-bearing rotation check outright. Those findings were deferred to D-127/D-128 and then the release candidate was built and evidence-captured with the failed assets in place.

The evidence is unambiguous. `docs/reviews/artifacts/T-079-crowd-03.png` — at the strategic zoom the spec says players will *routinely* use (§4) — renders **1,000 machines, 30 elites and a boss as a single 80×36-pixel grey smudge.** The horde is invisible.

And `T-079-close-05.png` shows why: at 128 px, in a labelled laboratory fixture, Tunneler / Transfer / Foundry / Breacher are four dark grey rounded rectangles in slightly different proportions. Same hue, same value, same soft baked-AO rendering. Only the Sapper has a distinct silhouette. At their in-game size of 4.4 px they are identical dots.

The root cause is the brief, not the generation. "Matte hull family, one cold accent, no glow, no outline, no tint" is a set of constraints that **guarantees** silhouette collapse. And §10.2 defines elites by the rule they break — nothing in any of these designs communicates *goes underground*, *hops a ring*, or *smashes walls*.

## 4. Screen by screen

### 4.1 Main menu — `release-start.png`

The strongest screen, and still not shippable.

- Broken toggles (RC-4). Three "Optional challenges" rows show a bare dot; the player cannot read on/off.
- Default dropdown chevrons on Doctrine, Loadout, Interface scale.
- The panel is a flat navy rounded rectangle with no material, no bezel, no relationship to the industrial art direction. It's a web form floating over a space picture.
- Left-edge alignment breaks between the wordmark, "Hold the star." and "Build its cage." — three different left margins within one lockup.
- A stray vertical rule with tick marks at x≈1115 (1440p) sits in the middle of the composition, orphaned. It renders differently at 1080p.
- Bottom navigation is six bare text labels evenly spaced. No affordance, no hover, no grouping, no icons.
- The background rings are a low-contrast brown band with a visibly tiling texture. They read as a dirty donut, not a megastructure.
- No motion anywhere. A main menu is the first promise a game makes.

### 4.2 First frame of a run — `release-opening.png`

This is what a player sees first, and it is the worst-composed screen in the build.

- **The fortress is not centred in the usable frame.** It sits at screen centre while the left 480 px is occupied by the build panel, so it reads as accidentally placed rather than composed.
- **The right 45 % of the screen is empty black.** Dense left, void right. Nothing occupies it — no threat log, no elite tracker, no objective panel.
- The star crowds ring 1 almost to the strut line; there is visually no room to build.
- Two "wing" objects at 9 and 4 o'clock protrude past the fortress silhouette on thin sticks and break the shape.
- The 12 o'clock object is at a wildly different scale from everything else and is lit from a different direction (RC-1).
- Tutorial text and selection state are stacked in the same orphan text block with no panel and no scrim, so at close zoom they render illegibly over bright ring art (visible in `release-detail.png`).
- The minimap is a decorative gold pie chart conveying nothing (see 4.5).

### 4.3 Combat / strategic view — `release-fortress.png`

- **Wedge boundaries are invisible.** The spec's central readability promise (§5: "twelve gives clock-face bearings, the most readable spatial callouts available in any language") is not delivered. You cannot see where wedge 12 ends and wedge 1 begins.
- **All rings look identical.** Same thickness, same texture, same colour at every radius. §5.1's entire tradeoff — cramped power-dense inner rings vs. spacious starved outer rings — has no visual expression at all.
- **Floating "2.0s" badges** in world space: an unstyled circled glyph plus text, six of them scattered around the fortress, unanchored and unexplained. They read as debug overlay.
- Turrets at strategic zoom are 6-pixel grey blobs. Flak and Mass Driver are indistinguishable. This is precisely the failure mode §15 predicted for gritty industrial.
- `1000.0M` energy — broken number formatting.
- The bottom objective line ("Containment 15:00 / Ignition") is orphan 20 px text, manually mis-centred.
- Elite identity is communicated by a wireframe N-gon outline drawn over the sprite (`live_view.gd:729-734`): triangle = tunneler, square = transfer, hexagon = foundry. A legend the player must memorise, drawn in a single cold colour, on top of art that already failed to differentiate.

### 4.4 Victory — `release-victory.png`

The emotional payoff of a fifteen-minute run is a spreadsheet with 45 % dead space.

- No hero shot of the fortress you built. No run graph. No peak-radius moment. No animation. No music cue beyond a 1.6 s sine chord.
- The number 65 appears three times in four lines: "Result saved. Credits: 65", "Earned reward: 65 credits (saved)", "Base reward 65".
- "0 Machines destroyed" on a victory screen.
- The four hero stats span the full panel width; the reward table below is compressed into the left half. Two incompatible grids on one screen, and the two-column reward table's value columns are ragged.
- Buttons are bottom-left orphans with a different primary/secondary treatment from the main menu's.
- Type sizes 48 / 20 / 64 / 24 with no consistent scale.

### 4.5 Ring status HUD — the minimap

`ring_status_hud.gd` is the mitigation the spec names for **Risk 3** (readability at scale) and **Risk 4** (relay frustration). It is currently unreadable, by construction:

- `fill_alpha` is **0.045** for healthy wedges and **0.14** for critical/broken (line 86). At 4.5 % opacity the status fill is invisible. The comment explains the intent — *"dark glass, an engraved bezel and bearing ticks establish an instrument rather than painting the entire clickable annulus as solid status colour"* — which is exactly the error: decoration was prioritised over the one job this widget has.
- `BAND_WIDTH = (max_radius - CORE_RADIUS) / bands`. At ring 12 with a ~72 px radius, each ring band is under 5 px tall and must encode 12 wedges. It cannot.
- Relay state is drawn but has no distinct visual weight, so Risk 4 ("a brownout the player doesn't understand is a bug report, not a mechanic") is live.

### 4.6 Settings and Controls

- Settings: right column ends at 57 % panel height leaving a large void; sliders have no numeric readout; `Controls` and `Back` are text-width buttons with no minimum width and no hierarchy.
- Controls: **26 bindings in one undifferentiated grid**, each rendered as `"Label: Key"` inside a single button. No keycap styling, no label/key columns, no group headers (Camera / Weapons / Structure / Terrain / Abilities / System). Reading order splits WASD across two rows.

### 4.7 Pause

The dim overlay extinguishes the star completely — the one element that should still burn. The title is left-aligned inside a centre-aligned dialog with full-width buttons. No blur, no run state (time remaining, rings held), no depth.

## 5. One design question that outranks all of the above

`run_rules.gd:6` — `const OPERATION_SECONDS := 900.0`, and `release-victory.png` reads **"Containment complete. The star is secure."**

The spec says (§1): *"endless run with persistent meta progression"* and *"The run ends when the last ring falls."* §2: *"the central stat of a run is your radius … one legible number the player watches climb and then watches fall."*

The shipped game is a **fixed fifteen-minute defence with a win state.** That is a different game. The "watch it climb, then watch it fall" arc — the thing §2 says must be protected in every future design decision — has been replaced by "survive a timer." It also explains why the victory screen feels hollow: there is nothing to celebrate, because you didn't build something remarkable, you waited.

I am not asserting the change is wrong. A bounded run is a legitimate choice for a v1 and may well be the right one. **I am asserting that nobody recorded it as a decision.** A change this load-bearing should be an explicit Tier 2 entry with Kevin's resolution, and it isn't. Astra should raise it as **D-129** and stop for an answer, because the polish plan below is scoped differently depending on which game this is.

## 6. Improvement plan

Six workstreams. **A, B and C are the ones that change the verdict** — if only those three ship, the game stops reading as amateur. D, E and F are what make it read as AA.

Owners follow BUILD-PLAN.md: **Sol** engine/performance, **Terra** rules/data, **Luna** presentation/input.

---

### Workstream A — Light the game *(highest leverage)*

**Proposed: T-081 · Owner Sol + Luna · Blocking: none**

1. Switch `renderer/rendering_method` from `gl_compatibility` to `mobile` or `forward_plus`. Re-run the performance suite immediately; this is the one change that could regress the frame budget, so measure before building on it.
2. Add a `WorldEnvironment` with glow enabled. Tune bloom threshold so running lights, weapon fire and the star's limb bloom, and nothing else does.
3. Add a `PointLight2D` at the core, radius scaled to the current ring count, colour driven by star palette and core HP. Give the ring, building and machine sprites normal maps so they receive it.
4. **Falloff is a gameplay channel, not just a look.** Inner rings should be visibly hot and bright; outer rings visibly cold and dim. That single change delivers §5.1's power-density tradeoff visually, for free, and it is currently delivered nowhere.
5. Re-light the building and machine sprites to a single canonical direction (radially outward from the core) so they stop disagreeing with each other.

**Exit check:** at strategic zoom, a screenshot of a 6-ring fortress shows a legible brightness gradient from ring 1 to ring 6, and weapon fire blooms. Frame time within 10 % of the RC2 baseline in `docs/reviews/presentation-v2/native-performance.txt`.

---

### Workstream B — Build the transient layer

**Proposed: T-082 (combat feel) · T-083 (ring collapse) · T-084 (UI motion) · Owner Luna · Blocking: A for glow**

**T-082 — Combat feel.** Every weapon gets: a muzzle flash, a tracer or beam with its own silhouette, an impact at the target, and a death effect for the machine. Five weapons, five distinguishable visual signatures. Add damage numbers (toggleable). Add a hit-flash on damaged wedges.

**T-083 — Ring collapse.** Replace `release_view.gd:500-505` outright. A ring collapse should be the most expensive-looking event in the game: structural break-up along wedge seams, debris on real trajectories, a light flash, a genuine screen shake, a hitch of slow-motion, and a distinct audio stinger. Spec §18 Q3 makes this an explicit validation gate — *"If question 3 comes back wrong, revisit total-loss collapse before building anything else"* — and it has never been honestly tested, because there was nothing to test.

**T-084 — Implement camera shake for real**, with a trauma-based decay model and a real noise source, so the "Reduced motion" toggle stops describing a feature that doesn't exist. Add UI motion: button press, panel enter/exit, tab transitions, HUD value count-ups, threat-alert pulse.

**Exit check:** side-by-side video of a ring collapse before and after. A player who has never seen the game can tell, with sound off, that something catastrophic happened.

---

### Workstream C — Commission real audio

**Proposed: T-085 · Owner Luna · Blocking: D-130 (budget/licensing — Kevin)**

`game_audio.gd` should be retired as a shipping system and kept only as a headless fallback.

- **Music:** minimum three states — menu, run (layered so intensity rises with threat), and boss. This is the largest single perceived-quality gain available per unit of effort in the entire plan.
- **SFX:** a real bank. Per-weapon fire and impact, per-machine death, elite arrival cues, wedge damage, wedge break, ring collapse, relay loss, brownout, build placement, build denied, and a full UI set (hover, click, back, toggle, error).
- **Randomisation:** pitch and sample variation on every repeated sound. Non-negotiable — the current fixed seed is the reason five minutes of play is unpleasant.
- **Mix:** proper buses, sidechain ducking of music under combat, and a low-core-HP filter sweep.
- **Positional audio** panned by bearing. In a game about omnidirectional pressure, hearing which side is failing is a genuine mechanic, and it directly mitigates Risk 3.

Kevin decides sourced-vs-commissioned and budget. Astra should raise this as **D-130** and not guess.

**Exit check:** five minutes of continuous play with headphones on is pleasant, and a player can identify which bearing is under attack with their eyes closed.

---

### Workstream D — Rebuild the UI as a design system

**Proposed: T-086 (theme) · T-087 (layout) · T-088 (screens) · Owner Luna · Blocking: none**

**T-086 — Real theme.** Expand `industrial_theme.gd` from 73 lines into a proper system:
- Custom `CheckButton` styleboxes **and checked/unchecked icons** — fix the broken toggles first, it is a one-hour fix with an outsized payoff.
- Custom `OptionButton` arrow, `PopupMenu`, `Tooltip`, `ScrollBar`, `ProgressBar`, `Tree`, `ItemList`.
- Distinct materials per widget class. A panel, a button and a slider must not share one `box()` call.
- A **type scale as named tokens** — display / title / heading / body / caption / mono-numeric — replacing the magic numbers in five files. One typeface. Delete every `ThemeDB.fallback_font` use.
- Focus, hover, pressed and disabled states on every interactive class, with controller focus visible.

**T-087 — Delete hard-coded pixel layout.** Convert `application.gd`, `live_view.gd` and `release_view.gd` to anchors and containers with a spacing scale (4/8/12/16/24/32/48). Then set `[display] stretch/mode = canvas_items` and let Godot handle resolution, which makes the "Interface scale" workaround unnecessary and makes 1080p, 1440p, 4K and ultrawide correct for free. Remove the `-220` centring fudge and every literal `Vector2(x,y)` placement.

**T-088 — Redesign the four weak screens** against the fixed system: main menu, first frame, victory, controls. Specifically:
- Main menu: one lockup with a single left margin, styled toggles, styled dropdowns, a bottom nav with real affordances, remove the orphan tick rule, and add motion.
- First frame: compose the fortress in the *usable* frame, and give the right side a job (threat/elite tracker, objective, recent events).
- Victory: a hero shot of the peak fortress, a radius-over-time graph, a single statement of the reward, one grid, and a proper button hierarchy.
- Controls: group headers, two-column label/keycap layout, real keycap styling.

**Exit check:** a screenshot of every screen at 1080p, 1440p and 21:9, with no misalignment, no default Godot widget, and no orphan text.

---

### Workstream E — Make the fortress and the swarm readable

**Proposed: T-089 (grid) · T-090 (swarm) · T-091 (minimap) · Owner Luna + Sol · Blocking: A**

**T-089 — The grid must read.** Draw wedge seams. Give ring 1 and ring 12 visibly different treatments — width, plating density, colour temperature, wear. Label bearings at the fortress edge (12/3/6/9 at minimum) so "eleven o'clock is collapsing" is a thing a player can actually say. Show wedge HP on the wedge, not only in the minimap.

**T-090 — Level-of-detail for the swarm.** This is the fix for the grey smudge in `T-079-crowd-03.png`. At strategic zoom, stop drawing sprites and draw **threat**: a density field or heat encoding around the perimeter that shows pressure per bearing, with elites promoted to distinct always-visible markers that never shrink below a legible floor. Elites are rule-breakers and must be individually trackable at every zoom. Sol will want this on a MultiMesh with a zoom-driven LOD switch.

**T-091 — Fix the minimap.** Raise `fill_alpha` from 0.045 to a value that is actually visible; the instrument bezel is not worth the information. Give ring bands a minimum height with scrolling or aggregation past ring 8. Make relay state and brownout impossible to miss — that is Risk 4, named in the spec.

**Exit check:** a screenshot at strategic zoom with 800+ machines where a reader can name which bearing is under most pressure and where the elites are, within two seconds.

---

### Workstream F — Redo the elite art brief

**Proposed: T-092 · Owner Astra (brief) + Luna (integration) · Blocking: D-131**

Do not regenerate against the existing brief; the brief is the bug.

- **Silhouette first.** Each elite must be identifiable as a black shape on white at 16 px. Design the silhouette, then render it. Test the silhouette before generating anything.
- **Form follows mechanic** (§10.2). Tunneler should look like it goes under — a drill, a low profile, something that reads as burrowing. Transfer should look like it jumps — thrusters, a coiled posture. Breacher should look like a ram. Foundry should look immovable. Sapper already works because it's the only one with a distinct shape; keep it.
- **Drop the no-glow / one-cold-accent constraint for elites.** It is what caused the collapse. Elites are rare and dangerous; they are allowed to be visually loud, and a self-illuminated accent is the cheapest legible differentiator at 4 px.
- Add a **minimum on-screen size floor** so an elite never renders below ~12 px regardless of zoom.
- Re-run the T-079 five-check gate. **Do not ship on a failed gate again** — that is the process fix, and it matters more than the assets.

Kevin owns the art-direction relaxation. Astra should raise it as **D-131**.

## 7. Sequencing

| Order | Workstream | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **D-129** (run structure), **D-130** (audio budget), **D-131** (elite art constraints) | Three decisions block real scope. Ask, then plan. |
| 1 | **A — Lighting** | Everything else looks better on top of it, and it carries the only performance risk. Do it first so the risk retires early. |
| 2 | **D-086 theme + T-087 layout** | Cheap, self-contained, and fixes the most visible amateur tells. The broken `CheckButton` alone is an hour. |
| 3 | **C — Audio** (long lead time, run in parallel from here) | Commissioning has latency; start it as early as the decision allows. |
| 4 | **B — Transient layer** | Needs lighting for glow. Biggest single feel gain. |
| 5 | **E — Readability** | Needs lighting for the ring gradient. |
| 6 | **T-088 screen redesigns** | Needs the theme and the layout system underneath. |
| 7 | **F — Elite art** | Longest lead, lowest coupling. Can run alongside everything from step 3. |

**The 20 % that gets 80 %:** style the `CheckButton`, switch the renderer and add glow, put a light at the core, replace the ring-collapse effect, and buy music. Those five items alone move this from "amateur" to "credible indie." The rest is the distance from credible to AA.

## 8. Process note for Astra

Two things went wrong at the process level and both are worth recording, because they will recur:

1. **A failed gate was deferred and then built upon.** T-079 said "the production acceptance gate does not pass." D-127/D-128 deferred the corrective pass. RC2 then shipped, captured release evidence, and was described as a release candidate — on top of the assets that failed. Deferring a gate is fine; *shipping through it* while still calling the result a candidate is not. Propose a standing rule: a failed acceptance gate blocks the release-candidate label until it is either passed or explicitly waived by Kevin in a recorded resolution.

2. **A spec-level design change was made without a decision entry.** The shift from §1's endless run to a 900-second win condition is exactly the kind of call CLAUDE.md reserves for Kevin, and it is not in DECISIONS.md. The bounded-run choice may be right; the missing entry is not.

Neither of these is a craft failure. Both are the reason a build that passes 39 test suites still reads as unfinished.
